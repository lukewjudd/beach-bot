# Maroubra north end: the point, the corner, and dated sessions

Compiled 9 Nov 2026. This adds to `eastern-reefs-dated.md` §4 and its notes on "Maroubra North End Point", and does not repeat them.
Already covered there: Wannasurf, the Surf Check YouTube north-corner entries, surfspots.co and thesurfatlas in brief.

**Bottom line:** the guides agree on the break itself: a left off the boulders at the north end, needing a bigger
N–NE (or E–NE) swell, a W–SW wind and mid tide. **I found no dated session that is clearly the point or reef itself.**
Every dated north-end observation is either the beach corner (the "Dunny Bowl" rip bowl) or a photo whose exact
peak can't be confirmed.

Blocked or unusable: swellnet.com (403 to fetches, so forecaster notes and comments could only be read through
search snippets or existing Wayback copies), surfline.com (403), yeeew.com (403), spots.guru (403), aquabumps.com
(bot challenge; read through existing Wayback copies), theinertia.com (403), swellarchive.com (429).
Magicseaweed has no archive. Swellnet's archived "Eastern Beaches" daily reports cover Bondi and never mention
Maroubra, and the archived North Maroubra cam pages have no report text.

## 1. What breaks at the north end, and where

Going north to south, Maroubra's north end has three distinct things (plus Lurline Bay round the corner, which is separate):

| Name(s) | What it is | Where | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Point / Mistral Point / "Maroubra North End Point"** | Left over boulders, rocks and sand. "A peaky takeoff over stone boulders gives a long left that opens up into a fatter shoulder as it runs inshore." Rides "generally under 50 metres" (mondo). "The northernmost take-off ... sucky peaks ripping off the stone boulders" (Surf Atlas). You get out by jumping off the north-end rocks and riding the rip. | Spot pin **-33.94595, 151.26008** (mondo.surf and surfspots.co use the same pin, probably copied from Wannasurf). That is about 150 m ENE of the north tip of the OSM Maroubra Beach polygon (-33.9452, 151.2587), on the rocks below Marine Parade. Mistral Point itself, the headland, is further NE at **-33.9415, 151.2653** (OSM node 325456848), and Mahon Pool is between them at -33.9430, 151.2639. So the "point" break is the boulder reef at the beach end of the headland, not the tip of Mistral Point. | https://www.surfspots.co/spot/maroubra-north-end-point ; https://www.mondo.surf/surf-spot/maroubra-north-end-point/guide/9952 ; https://thesurfatlas.com/australia-surfing/surfing-in-maroubra/ ; OSM Nominatim |
| **Dunny Bowl / North End / the "Colosseum"** | The rip bowl in the **north corner of the beach**, over sand, with "sharper rights and longer lefts". **It is at the north end, not the south.** Three explanations of the name: the permanent rip "swirls like a flushing toilet" (ABC, quoting local lifeguards); it breaks "in front of the toilet block" (Surf Bugle); or "in front of an old sewage pipe" (The Inertia, via search snippet). Council confirms there is a north-end toilet block. Tracks calls the North End an "amphitheatre setup" watched from the car park above, and a Swellnet commenter calls the cove the "Colosseum". | North corner of the sand, just south of the point. The rip runs out along the north rocks. | https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-08-13/cove-forms-at-sydneys-maroubra-beach/104214938 ; https://surfbugle.substack.com/p/cliff-the-cunt-and-worlds-most-localised ; https://tracksmag.com.au/love-thy-local-maroubra-462301 ; https://www.swellnet.com/news/swellnet-analysis/2026/02/20/the-finite-shore (comment 21 Feb 2026) |
| **Stromies / "Stormy" / Stormy Left** | "The last of the reef breaks that string along the northern end of Maroubra ... a punchy peak that forms about 200 meters of[f] the rocks" (Surf Atlas). The Swellnet commenter remembers "the Stormy Left was super consistent and the prime wave" in the 1970s–80s. | About 200 m south of the north rocks (no coordinates given). | Surf Atlas; Swellnet comment above |

Other names checked:
- **"Bombora" / "the Bombie" at Maroubra:** no source names one. The Surf Atlas mentions "outer reefs that hug this part of
  Maroubra Beach" along the north end, with no name.
- **Pumphouse:** surfspots.co lists a left reef "Pumphouse" 0.51 mi (about 0.8 km) from the North End Point pin, with no
  direction, so it may be at Lurline or on the Mistral side. It was not investigated further.
- **Lurline Bay** is north of Mistral Point and separate. It is already a `SurfSpot` in `beaches.py` and is not covered here.
- **"North Maroubra"** is used loosely for the whole north half of the beach: the Swellnet "North Maroubra" cam, the
  North Maroubra Surf Riders club, and ABC's "It comes into North Maroubra pretty hard". It does not name the point specifically.
- The National Surfing Reserve page (declared March 2006) gives no per-break detail, just "reef pockets at the north and south corners
  ... left and right beach breaks and a left point break". The nomination document is not online.
  https://www.surfingreserves.org/?p=25

## 2. Conditions it needs, per source

### The Point (North End Point)

| Source | Swell dir | Size | Wind | Tide | Other |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wannasurf (already in notes) | N–NE | 1–1.5 m to 3 m+ | W–SW | mid | experienced; localism |
| mondo.surf | N–NE (337.5–67.5°) | "Autumn's north to northeast swells of about 1 to 1.5 m are the sweet spot"; handles nearly 3 m | SW–W (202.5–292.5°) | mid | intermediate (disagrees with Wannasurf's "experienced"); rocks and sand; "regional classic". Reads like a rewrite of Wannasurf. |

| Surf Atlas | "strong NE swells" | — | "best protection from summer N winds, so is popular November-March" | — | — |

| yeeew (search snippet only, page 403) | — | "only really works on the bigger swells" | — | — | "left-hand reef breaking wave off the rocks" |
| surf-forecast.com (whole beach) | NE | — | NW | all tides | mentions "a left hand point break" within the Maroubra Beach entry, with no separate spot |

**Agreement:** a left; NE in the swell; westerly wind (W–SW, or NW for the whole beach); mid tide; it needs some size (about 1 m+, best 1–1.5 m, holds about 3 m).

**Disagreements:**
- Swell: N–NE (Wannasurf, mondo, xtremespots) against E–NE (surfspots.co). Since the beach faces about 90°, a true N
  swell barely reaches it. The NE to ENE band seems the safer reading.
- Wind: W–SW against NW. The north corner sits in the lee of Mistral Point, so a NW–N wind is fine there (Surf Atlas:
  N-wind shelter in summer). A SW is fine too, because it is offshore to cross-offshore on an east-facing beach. In big S
  swells with **southerlies**, though, the north end is reported as "a mess" (Surf Check 2024-08-02, 2025-07-04).
- Level: experienced (Wannasurf, xtremespots) against intermediate (mondo).
- Period: **no source gives a period.**

### Dunny Bowl / the north corner (beach)

- Surf Atlas: "Strong 5-10 foot winter swells with SE or direct E can turn it into a frothing A-frame peak ... fires
  even when it's really big and can hold up when other parts of the Sydney beaches are blown out." "The paddle can be
  hard and rips are strong."
- Surf Bugle / Inertia: "the most consistent, and best, of the waves is in the northern end": a rip bowl with sharper rights and longer lefts.
- Tracks 2017: "Three-foot, offshore and doing what Dunny Bowl does", until "the wind went south and destroyed it".
- Swellnet commenter (21 Feb 2026), about the 1970s–80s: "Big peaks broke within the north end cove (what I call the
  'Colosseum'). There was always the deep rip along the rocks, with right handers (any size) towards the rocks". Today:
  "whenever I am there to observe a solid swell, there appears so much sand stacked up beyond the headland its mostly
  an unsurfable close out when it has some size."
- ABC, 13 Aug 2024: big winter **south** swells strip the sand from the north corner and leave a rocky cove, with a stronger rip and harder entry and exit.
  The local photographer Glenn Duffus says the moved sand makes it harder to catch waves at the northern end, and that this happens every year
  after a big winter S swell. The sand comes back over about 2 weeks to 2 months once it goes flat.

**Synthesis (my reading, not a source's):** the north end has two regimes.
1. **NE–ENE swell, about 1–3 m, W–NW wind, mid tide:** the **point left** wraps off the boulders and runs south down the beach.
2. **E–SE swell:** the **corner bowl** throws A-frames, with **rights running north into the rocks**. This matches the Surf Check
   "sucky rights in the north corner" on 2025-04-08/09 and the commenter's "right handers towards the rocks". It needs a W
   wind; southerlies ruin it.

After big S swells the sand is stripped and the corner gets worse for weeks.

## 4. Implications for the bot (suggestions)

- Ground truth for the point itself is effectively nil. If it becomes its own `SurfSpot`, its parameters rest on
  guidebook consensus alone: dirs NE (and ENE) weighted highest, E moderate, SE/S low; size about 1–3 m; W–NW–SW wind fine; mid
  tide. Be explicit in comments that it is unvalidated.
- The north **corner** (Dunny Bowl) behaves differently: it suits E–SE, is ruined by S winds, and degrades for weeks
  after big winter S swells strip the sand. Any backtest using Surf Check "north corner" days is testing this corner, not the point.
- Candidate days to check the point against archived forecasts: summer NE swell days with a W–NW morning wind. None are confirmed.
